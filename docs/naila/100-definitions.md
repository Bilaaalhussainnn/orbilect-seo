## robots.txt

A robots.txt file gives cooperating crawlers instructions about which paths they may fetch. It is not a password system, and blocking crawling alone does not reliably remove a URL from search results.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## XML sitemap

An XML sitemap lists preferred public URLs to help search engines discover a site’s content. Submitting it supports discovery but does not guarantee that every URL will be indexed.

Related page: /blogs/how-to-create-and-submit-an-xml-sitemap

## Schema

Schema is a shared vocabulary for describing things such as an organization, article, or product in machine-readable data. Markup should describe the visible content accurately; it does not guarantee a special search appearance.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Canonical URL

A canonical URL is the preferred address for a page among duplicate or very similar versions. A canonical annotation is a signal rather than an instruction that search engines must always follow.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Crawling

Crawling is how a search engine fetches pages and follows links to discover content. A page must be reachable before its content can be evaluated for search, although crawling and indexing are different stages.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Indexing

Indexing is the process of analyzing and storing information about a page so it may be considered for search results. Being indexed does not guarantee a particular position or traffic level.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Noindex

Noindex tells supporting search engines not to include a page in their search index. The crawler generally needs access to the page to see this directive, so a robots.txt block can prevent it from being read.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Nofollow

Nofollow is a link relationship value used to qualify a link’s relationship to its destination. It is different from noindex, which concerns whether a page should appear in a search index.

Related page: /services/off-page-seo

## Backlink

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Relevance, context, and legitimacy matter; simply accumulating a large number of links is not a reliable strategy for useful visibility.

Related page: /services/off-page-seo

## Anchor text

Anchor text is the visible wording of a link. Descriptive wording helps people understand the destination, whereas repeated vague labels provide less context about what the linked page contains.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Internal link

An internal link connects pages on the same website. Useful internal links help visitors navigate related information and help crawlers discover how the site’s pages relate to each other.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## External link

An external link points from your website to a different website. Link to relevant, trustworthy sources when they help the reader verify a claim or continue a useful task.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Keyword

A keyword is a word or phrase used to describe a search topic or target an advertising opportunity. Good research considers the intent and business relevance behind a phrase rather than treating volume as the only criterion.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Search intent

Search intent is the task or need behind a query, such as learning, comparing, finding a location, or buying. A useful page should fit that task instead of merely repeating the query’s words.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Long-tail keyword

A long-tail keyword is a more specific search phrase within a broader topic. Specific phrases can reveal a clearer customer need, but they are not automatically easy to rank for or commercially valuable.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## SERP

SERP means search engine results page. Its layout can include organic links, ads, local results, images, and other features depending on the query and the search system.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## CTR

Click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions, usually expressed as a percentage. Compare it within the same channel, audience, and reporting context rather than assuming every placement should achieve the same rate.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## Impression

An impression records an eligible display or appearance under the reporting system’s rules. An impression is not the same as a visit, a click, or proof that someone carefully read the content.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## Organic traffic

Organic traffic is traffic attributed to unpaid search results in the reporting setup being used. Classification and consent settings can affect analytics totals, so compare them carefully with Search Console clicks.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Local SEO

Local SEO helps eligible businesses be discovered for relevant location-based searches. It can involve accurate business information, useful location pages, appropriate profiles, and a clear route to calling or booking.

Related page: /industries

## Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile is Google’s system for eligible businesses to manage public business information in its local experiences. Eligibility, verification, categories, and profile details should reflect the real business.

Related page: /blogs/how-to-set-up-your-google-business-profile

## On-page SEO

On-page SEO improves the content and structure of individual pages, including titles, headings, links, and useful explanations. It starts with what the visitor needs to understand and do.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Off-page SEO

Off-page SEO concerns relevant signals and visibility beyond your own website, including legitimate links, mentions, and listings. Quality and accuracy matter more than indiscriminate submissions or promised link counts.

Related page: /services/off-page-seo

## Technical SEO

Technical SEO addresses how search engines access, understand, and index a website. Work can include redirects, canonicals, structured data, rendering, sitemaps, and performance diagnostics.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Ecommerce SEO

Ecommerce SEO improves discovery of products and collections through useful product data, clear categories, internal links, and technical handling of variants and filters. The shopping journey still needs to work well on mobile.

Related page: /services/ecommerce-seo

## Hreflang

Hreflang annotations identify language or regional alternatives of related pages. They help search systems understand which version may suit a user, but they do not replace translation or a coherent international site structure.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Alt text

Alt text describes the purpose or information conveyed by an image for people who cannot see it. Decorative images can use empty alternative text; keyword stuffing makes descriptions less useful.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Title tag

A title tag supplies a page’s HTML title and can help identify it in browser tabs and search. Write a specific, accurate title; search engines can choose different title text for a result.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Meta description

A meta description is a short HTML summary of a page. It can inform a search snippet, but the search engine may generate another snippet depending on the query and page content.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## URL slug

A URL slug is the readable path segment identifying a page, such as website-redesign. Keep it understandable and plan redirects when changing established URLs that visitors or search engines already use.

Related page: /services/website-redesign

## Redirect

A redirect sends a request from one URL to another. Use the appropriate status for a permanent or temporary move and direct old pages to a relevant replacement rather than always to the homepage.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## HTTP status code

An HTTP status code describes the result of a request, such as success, redirection, not found, or a server error. Diagnose the actual production response rather than assuming a page’s appearance proves its status.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Structured data

Structured data is machine-readable information about a page’s content. JSON-LD is one common format for publishing it, and the facts in the markup should agree with the visible page.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Rich result

A rich result is a search appearance enhanced with supported structured information or other features. Valid markup can support eligibility, but the search engine decides whether and how an enhancement is displayed.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## FAQ markup

FAQ markup describes published questions and their answers in structured data. Having an FAQ section does not mean a website will receive an FAQ enhancement in search results.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Organization schema

Organization schema describes an organization using structured properties such as its name, URL, and logo. The information should be accurate and consistent with the business’s public identity.

Related page: /about

## Breadcrumb

Breadcrumb navigation shows a page’s place within a site hierarchy. It can help navigation on some sites; a website does not need to display breadcrumbs merely to use a consistent information architecture.

Related page: /services/website-redesign

## Core Web Vitals

Core Web Vitals describe aspects of loading, interaction responsiveness, and visual stability. Review them alongside accessibility, useful content, and whether customers can complete the tasks that matter.

Related page: /blogs/what-core-web-vitals-tell-you-about-your-website

## LCP

Largest Contentful Paint measures when the largest eligible visible content element is rendered. Large images, server response time, and rendering delays can influence it, so identify the actual element before choosing a fix.

Related page: /blogs/what-core-web-vitals-tell-you-about-your-website

## INP

Interaction to Next Paint measures a page’s responsiveness to user interactions. Long JavaScript tasks and expensive rendering can delay visible feedback even after the initial page has loaded.

Related page: /blogs/what-core-web-vitals-tell-you-about-your-website

## CLS

Cumulative Layout Shift measures unexpected movement of visible content. Reserving space for images, advertisements, and embeds can reduce movement while a page loads and updates.

Related page: /blogs/what-core-web-vitals-tell-you-about-your-website

## Page speed

Page speed concerns how quickly useful content and interactions become available. Measure a real production page with its images, scripts, and integrations instead of relying on one simplified preview.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Duplicate content

Duplicate content is substantially similar material accessible at more than one URL. Clarify the intended canonical structure and consolidate overlapping pages when separate versions do not serve distinct visitor needs.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Crawl budget

Crawl budget describes the crawling attention available to a site in context. For many small sites, fixing access, quality, and URL duplication is more useful than treating crawl budget as the first problem.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Rendering

Rendering is the process of turning HTML, styles, scripts, and assets into the page a browser or supported crawler can interpret. Important content should remain accessible through the site’s chosen rendering architecture.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## JavaScript SEO

JavaScript SEO examines how script-driven websites expose content, links, and metadata to search systems. Check the rendered production output and avoid relying on interactions a crawler may never perform.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Orphan page

An orphan page has no useful internal links leading to it from the site’s navigable structure. Add relevant links when the page belongs in the customer journey rather than relying only on a sitemap.

Related page: /services/on-page-seo

## Faceted navigation

Faceted navigation lets shoppers filter a catalogue by attributes such as size, brand, or price. Plan which filter combinations should be search destinations to avoid an uncontrolled collection of duplicate parameter URLs.

Related page: /services/ecommerce-seo

## Robots meta tag

A robots meta tag publishes page-level instructions for supporting crawlers, such as noindex. It differs from robots.txt, which controls crawling paths rather than describing indexing instructions inside a page.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## Googlebot

Googlebot is the name used for Google’s search crawlers. A user-agent string alone does not prove a request is genuine, so server-side verification needs the appropriate documented checks.

Related page: /services/technical-seo

## PPC

PPC means pay-per-click advertising, where clicks are one basis for charging. A successful campaign also needs relevant targeting, a useful destination, accurate measurement, and a viable offer.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## ROAS

Return on ad spend compares attributed revenue with advertising spend. It is not profit because product costs, fulfilment, management fees, and other expenses may still need to be deducted.

Related page: /blogs/how-to-tell-whether-ppc-is-profitable

## ROI

Return on investment compares a return or gain with the investment required under a clearly defined calculation. State which costs and time period are included before comparing campaigns or channels.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## CPC

Cost per click is the advertising cost divided by recorded clicks for the period. A low CPC is useful only when those clicks are relevant and contribute to the intended business outcome.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## CPM

CPM expresses advertising cost per thousand impressions. Compare it within suitable placement and audience contexts, and do not treat an impression total as a measure of completed sales.

Related page: /services/paid-social-advertising

## CPA

Cost per acquisition divides the relevant advertising cost by the counted acquisition actions. Define the action carefully because an enquiry, qualified lead, and paying customer are different outcomes.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## CPL

Cost per lead compares campaign cost with the leads counted under an agreed definition. Follow qualification and sales outcomes as well, since inexpensive enquiries are not necessarily useful opportunities.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## Conversion rate

Conversion rate is the proportion of a defined population that completes a chosen action. State the denominator and action clearly, such as successful enquiries per session, before comparing two reports.

Related page: /services/conversion-rate-optimization

## Quality Score

Quality Score is a Google Ads diagnostic associated with expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing-page experience. Use it to investigate relevance rather than treating the score itself as a business outcome.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## Ad Rank

Ad Rank is used by Google Ads to help determine ad eligibility and position using several factors. A larger bid alone does not guarantee a particular placement or profitable traffic.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## Negative keyword

Negative keywords help prevent ads from matching unwanted searches under the platform’s rules. Review the actual search intent before excluding phrases that may also contain useful demand.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## Keyword match type

A keyword match type controls how an advertising keyword can relate to searches under the platform’s matching system. Review search terms and current platform documentation rather than assuming matching is only literal text equality.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## Attribution

Attribution assigns credit for outcomes across interactions according to a model and available data. Different systems can report different totals because their definitions, windows, and observed journeys differ.

Related page: /services/marketing-analytics

## Remarketing

Remarketing reaches eligible audiences based on earlier interactions, subject to platform rules and applicable consent requirements. It needs thoughtful audience definitions, useful messaging, and appropriate data handling.

Related page: /services/paid-social-advertising

## Ad group

An ad group organizes related advertisements and targeting within a campaign. A clear structure helps match the message and destination to the audience or search intent being served.

Related page: /services/ppc-management

## Campaign

A campaign groups advertising activity around settings and objectives such as budget and delivery. Define the commercial goal and conversion measurement before judging performance from reach or clicks alone.

## Ad spend

Ad spend is the amount paid to the advertising platform for delivery under its billing rules. It is usually separate from agency management, design, landing-page work, and other service costs unless the quote says otherwise.
