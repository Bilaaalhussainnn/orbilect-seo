## What each term describes

SEO focuses on discovery and visibility in unpaid search. AEO is a shorthand for making answers easy to identify and use. GEO is a shorthand for improving the chance that a generative experience can find, interpret or cite useful material. The labels overlap, and platforms can implement these experiences differently.

### Map each approach to a specific discovery moment

Traditional SEO helps your pages become eligible for relevant unpaid results. Answer engine optimization, or AEO, describes making an answer easy to extract and use. Generative engine optimization, or GEO, describes creating useful, attributable material that may be surfaced by generative systems. These are industry terms with overlapping practices. Give a page a real purpose before deciding which label describes the work.

One content set for different search tasks

1. **Find**Create a discoverable page for a real need
2. **Answer**Put a clear response beside its supporting detail
3. **Reference**Publish original evidence with transparent sourcing

## What to do on the page

Publish a clear answer backed by evidence. Put the method, author, limits and date close to claims that depend on them. Keep material in accessible HTML and make related pages easy to find. Google says its AI search features build on its general SEO practices.

### Write an answer that survives being read in isolation

Answer the question near its heading. Then explain the conditions, source, examples and exceptions. On a supplier page, a delivery promise is useful only if it states the geography, cutoff and exclusions. A comparison guide can add original criteria and a dated methodology. Google’s guidance for its AI search features keeps the same core SEO fundamentals and does not require special AI markup.

## Measure outcomes across formats

Track qualified organic visits and conversions as usual. Where referrals are identifiable, compare them separately. Sample the questions customers actually ask and track whether your pages are referenced. Treat mentions, clicks and revenue as distinct measures.

### Measure each surface using evidence you can reproduce

Keep a small set of queries for each major task, such as comparison, definition or purchase. Record the date and context when manually checking AI references because answers vary. Follow visits and enquiries from known referrers where analytics can distinguish them. Use Search Console for Google Search performance, but avoid claiming you can isolate every visit from AI features in its existing reports.

## Start with the customer’s question

List the decisions customers must make before contacting or buying. Write a direct explanation first, then add examples, limitations, and evidence. Calling the work AEO or GEO does not replace a page that answers a specific question clearly.

## Make important claims easy to verify

Link factual statements to their original source and distinguish your calculations from external statistics. Explain who wrote or reviewed specialist material. A clear citation trail helps readers evaluate the page without suggesting that a search engine must cite it.

## Keep technical access straightforward

Serve meaningful content in accessible HTML, maintain canonical URLs, and link related pages logically. Review crawler permissions against the business’s publishing choices. Do not create special AI files or markup under the assumption that they guarantee inclusion in generated answers.

## Evaluate visibility through several signals

Track search impressions, relevant referral traffic, branded searches, and qualified enquiries together. Generated-answer visibility can vary by query and session. Record the prompt, date, and observed citation when checking an AI answer rather than treating a single screenshot as permanent placement.

## Avoid replacing expertise with volume

Improve useful pages before producing hundreds of similar answers. Add original observations, a practical method, or a carefully labelled worked example. Publish fewer claims when evidence is weak and schedule reviews for subjects whose tools or rules change frequently.

## Separate a direct answer from the evidence supporting it

A concise definition helps a reader orient themselves, but the rest of the page should explain its scope, examples and limits. For a service topic, distinguish what the term means from how your team applies it and which outcomes remain outside your control.

This structure supports both quick understanding and deeper evaluation. It is an editorial recommendation, not a special format that guarantees a featured answer, an AI citation or a particular search presentation.

## Avoid creating several near-identical pages for new labels

Before launching separate SEO, AEO and GEO landing pages, compare the audience, deliverables and underlying information. If the pages say the same thing with different acronyms, a single stronger explanation may be more useful.

Separate pages make sense when they address genuinely different questions or services. Give each one a distinct purpose and connect them with relevant links, instead of expecting additional labels alone to create additional search demand.

## Keep machine-readable claims consistent with visible content

If a page describes a service, organization or article through structured data, ensure the same facts appear clearly for visitors. Do not add invisible ratings, invented awards or unsupported expertise to make the page look more authoritative to an automated system.

Google's AI search guidance does not prescribe a special AI schema. Start with eligible, accessible content and accurate existing markup, then evaluate whether people can verify the actual claims on the page.

## Use a repeatable observation method for AI visibility

When checking whether an answer engine references your site, record the question, date, context and destination URL. Treat the result as an observation from that session, not proof that every user receives the same answer.

Combine those observations with referral traffic and real enquiries where measurable. A screenshot of one citation is useful evidence of that occurrence, but it cannot establish the total reach, commercial value or permanence of an AI feature.

## One topic across three formats

A commercial solar installer creates a service page for business buyers, a direct explanation of the site assessment and a guide comparing financing options. The service page serves demand, the direct explanation helps users with a specific question, and the financing guide offers factual context worth referencing. Links connect related material where a reader will need it.

A useful editorial brief can track all three tasks without creating three versions of the same article. Keep one preferred URL, assign an owner to confirm facts, and define which claims need their own source or example. When revising a page, compare the opening answer and supporting detail together. A clear definition helps readers enter the page. Original examples and transparent limitations give them a reason to keep reading or share the page with somebody making the same decision.

Choose the format for the customer task

| Task | Useful format | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a supplier | Service page with territory and criteria | Relevant clicks and enquiries |
| Get one answer | Direct explanation with conditions | Can readers resolve the question quickly? |
| Compare options | Sourced comparison with criteria | Sources, referrals and useful next steps |

## Common questions

### Do I need a separate website version for AI search?

Google says ordinary SEO requirements apply to its AI features. Start with crawlable, helpful pages and keep material claims easy to verify.

### Should I publish an FAQ for every keyword?

Group genuinely related customer questions on the page that serves their needs. Add a separate guide if the question requires enough explanation to stand on its own.

### Are these separate services on every project?

They can be delivered in the same content and technical programme. Decide which discovery problems you need to solve before buying an additional workstream for the same pages.
