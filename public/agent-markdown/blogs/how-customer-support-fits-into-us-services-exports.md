## Identify who sells and who buys

A U.S. resident business selling remote customer support to a non-U.S. customer may be supplying a U.S. service export. A U.S. company buying support from a provider abroad is generally importing the service from its perspective. The caller’s location alone does not determine the transaction direction.

### Define the direction of the service contract

A service export generally involves a US resident supplying services to a foreign resident under the relevant statistical definition. When a US firm buys support from an independently located foreign provider, the transaction can instead be a US service import. A foreign affiliate’s local activity is a separate question. Before citing a figure, check whether it covers cross-border transactions, affiliate activity or a different reporting basis.

## Distinguish trade from foreign affiliate sales

Remote delivery is one way to supply services across borders. Sales through a foreign affiliate can be measured separately. For a multi-entity contract, establish who contracts, who pays and which entities operate in the countries involved.

### Read categories before treating a dataset as a market estimate

Customer support can be part of broader business, technical or administrative service bundles. Identify the reporting category and what actually belongs in it before citing a number. A table for one broad category cannot establish a call-centre market size. Compare years and countries using the same definitions, and note revisions or aggregation that limit interpretation.

## Measure the contract operationally

Set expectations for resolution time, handoffs, quality review and peak coverage. Separate contact volume from resolved issues and document who owns data access, escalation and reporting. A shared definition of a resolved case helps both organisations compare performance.

### Separate economic reporting from outsourcing decisions

A buyer deciding where to locate support should consider coverage windows, languages, agent quality, product training and information access. Ask how escalations, audits and continuity will work after launch. Report operating measures such as resolution time and customer feedback separately from the value of the contracted service.

## Define the transaction before classifying it

Identify the supplier, customer, service activity, and countries involved. Cross-border customer support can be part of a services transaction, but classification depends on the facts. Use the relevant statistical definitions rather than treating every remotely delivered support task as the same category.

## Separate commercial reporting from statistics

A company’s export revenue, a balance-of-payments category, and a marketing description answer different questions. Explain which measure an article or report uses. Avoid combining totals from incompatible datasets or implying that a broad services category measures the entire BPO market directly.

## Make delivery responsibilities explicit

Document staffing coverage, quality review, escalation, data access, and continuity arrangements. A cross-border service still needs a clear operating model. Define who owns customer records and which actions support agents can take before granting access to production systems.

## Use transparent unit economics

Compare service fees with supervision, technology, training, and handover costs. A low hourly rate does not establish the cost per resolved case. Use a labelled example calculation and include the assumptions required to compare two delivery models fairly.

## Read trade figures with their methodology

Check the period, units, revision status, geography, and service definition before quoting a total. Link to the original dataset or methodology. Keep operational observations separate from national statistics so readers can see what the evidence does and does not establish.

## Identify the resident supplier before describing an export

The customer's language, telephone number or brand name does not determine the direction of international trade. Start with the residence of the supplier and buyer, and distinguish a cross-border service transaction from activity conducted through an affiliate.

For example, support sold by a non-US resident provider to a US resident business should not casually be described as a US export. Use the relevant statistical definitions when explaining the transaction rather than borrowing an impressive national headline.

## Separate contract delivery from the statistical service category

A commercial support agreement may contain customer service, technical troubleshooting, software access and management activity together. Those practical workstreams do not automatically identify one statistical reporting category.

Record what is actually supplied and use the applicable reporting guidance if a formal classification is needed. A marketing description of the team is not a substitute for the definitions used by the agency collecting trade statistics or by the business preparing an official return.

[BEA: international trade in services data](https://www.bea.gov/itable/international-transactions-services-and-investment-position)

## Describe service quality using contract evidence

Use operational measures the contract can support, such as coverage, response handling, quality review and completion of agreed tasks. State how each measure is defined and which system records it.

Distinguish the team's delivery evidence from national services-trade totals. A large market figure cannot establish the quality, security or commercial value of a particular outsourcing arrangement, and a provider should be able to explain its own operating model without relying on unrelated statistics.

## Keep the operating handover clear across borders

Document the customer systems used, approved access, supervision, escalation and the handling of changes in coverage or workload. Assign ownership on both sides so an unresolved issue does not disappear between time zones.

Review the arrangement after real work begins. The business should know which requests the support team can complete, which decisions need approval and how service continuity is maintained when a staff member, tool or customer requirement changes.

## Who is exporting in two contract examples?

If a US-based support vendor supplies support to a customer based abroad, the transaction can be a US services export. If an overseas firm supplies support under contract to a US customer, it can be a US import. A US parent company with an overseas affiliate raises separate questions about affiliates and the parties to the transaction.

When comparing an outsourcing proposal with public data, keep the two units of analysis separate. A statement about national exports can describe the direction and value of trade, while a buying decision depends on the contract, staffing model, language coverage and obligations for handling information. Document how the provider will measure workload, holidays and escalations. The contract can then say which service levels apply and how both parties will verify them.

Interpret the transaction before quoting trade data

| Scenario | Evidence to find | Reporting question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US provider serves foreign client | Residence and party to the contract | Is the US party providing the service abroad? |
| Foreign provider serves US client | Residence and contracting records | Is the US party buying the service from abroad? |
| Foreign affiliate serves local client | Ownership and place of activity | Does the data source report sales through affiliates? |

## Common questions

### Does the language of support determine whether it is a US export?

No. Check the residence and relationship of the provider and customer and which statistical measure is being used.

### Where should I start when quoting industry figures?

Start with the BEA table and definitions. Cite the relevant line, year, basis and any limitations rather than assigning the whole category to customer support.

### Can time-zone coverage tell me the country of export?

No. The coverage window is an operating choice. Use the relevant statistical definitions and the residence of the actual contracting parties.
