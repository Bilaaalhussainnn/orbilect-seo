## Decide what needs review

Use AI for outlines, variations or summaries when helpful. Assign a human owner to verify claims, instructions and source references. Escalate safety-sensitive or technical material to someone with relevant expertise.

### Set the publication standard before generating a draft

Give the writer and reviewers a brief that defines audience, purpose, evidence, tone and who approves publication. Use assistants to explore outlines, identify unclear passages or organise material you can verify. Keep original customer information out of unapproved tools. Assign an owner for unsupported statements, invented citations and stale policies before content goes live.

A content review from draft to publication

1. **Assign the brief**Define the audience, owner and evidence standard
2. **Verify the claims**Check the primary sources and test risky steps
3. **Approve and revisit**Record decisions and update when evidence changes

## Check against the original evidence

Open the primary sources and confirm dates, figures and interface names. Try the steps when producing a tutorial. Replace invented examples with labelled hypothetical scenarios or documented real examples.

### Review claims according to the cost of getting them wrong

Check dates, numbers, quotes and links at the original source. Test instructions where possible and identify the platform version or market they cover. Ask a subject specialist to approve statements where readers could make significant business, financial, clinical or operational decisions. Smooth prose is not proof: record the source and the decision for each consequential claim.

## Keep editorial accountability after publication

Store source notes and identify who will review the page when it becomes outdated. Add useful first-hand details and improve the page when readers flag unclear sections. Review results with the same standards used for other content.

### Build feedback into the next revision

Review the draft for usefulness after checking accuracy. Ask whether someone could complete a task from the page, what examples are missing and where the author relies on assumptions. Keep a small style and fact sheet for common topics. After publication, watch for reader questions, policy changes and broken links and assign the next review date when the information is likely to change.

## Assign responsibility for factual claims

A named editor should verify claims against the original source, especially for health, finance, law, or product limitations. AI output is draft material rather than evidence. Remove a statement when its basis cannot be established instead of adding a vague citation.

## Check usefulness beyond grammatical quality

Ask whether the article answers a real reader question with specific, actionable information. Smooth prose can still be repetitive or empty. Add relevant examples, decision criteria, and limitations based on genuine knowledge rather than padding the page to reach a word count.

## Review originality and source handling

Check copied phrasing, fabricated references, and unsupported quotations. Summarize sources in your own words and link to the originals. Clearly distinguish the author’s interpretation from a study’s findings so readers can evaluate the argument without guessing who made each claim.

## Test the page as a published experience

Inspect headings, tables, image descriptions, mobile layout, and links after publication. Confirm that structured data reflects the visible article. An editorial review should include the rendered page because formatting and implementation can change what the reader actually receives.

## Maintain a review record

Keep the source list, editor, publication date, and material revision history. Revisit content when its underlying facts change. Do not change the date merely to create an appearance of freshness; record meaningful updates that affect the usefulness or accuracy of the article.

## Review claims at the sentence level

Identify statements about prices, product capabilities, policies, statistics and outcomes, then check them against the appropriate original evidence. A paragraph can sound coherent while containing one unsupported detail that changes the reader's understanding.

Keep a source note for claims likely to need future maintenance. The reviewer should be able to distinguish verified facts from examples, interpretation and promotional language instead of approving the whole draft because its tone feels professional.

## Test whether the content adds anything useful

Compare the draft with the reader's actual task. Look for missing instructions, vague examples and passages that repeat a definition without helping someone decide or act.

Replace generic material with relevant explanations or remove it. Google's guidance on AI-assisted content emphasizes accuracy, quality and relevance; increasing the quantity of polished sentences does not by itself create additional value for the person who arrives from search.

## Check the final page rather than only the document

Review the published headings, source links, image descriptions, tables and calls to action after the content enters the website template. Errors can appear during formatting even when the approved draft is correct.

Make sure a chart or screenshot supports the nearby explanation and that an example is labelled appropriately. A final page review also catches broken links, inaccessible tables and an enquiry route that no longer matches the service described.

## Assign a reviewer when the underlying facts change

Give time-sensitive pages an owner and a reason to revisit them, such as a platform update, product release or revised business policy. An updated date should reflect a substantive review or change rather than an automatic daily refresh.

Keep the review record concise: what was checked, which sources changed and what was corrected. This makes ongoing maintenance possible without relying on the original writer to remember how every statement was produced.

## Editorial review of a payment guide

A draft says a payment feature is available worldwide. The editor finds that the official instructions depend on the merchant’s market. A reviewer tests setup in a supported account, rewrites the section around the applicable market and links to the eligibility rules so readers can check their own situation.

Separate the approval record from the published prose. Capture links, access dates, screenshots of controlled tests when appropriate, reviewer comments and the final decision in your editorial workflow. Commit to an update if the page relies on a changing interface or rule. A search-led site may receive readers months after a draft was written, so give readers a way to tell when evidence was checked and which version of the instructions they can expect.

Assign review based on the type of claim

| Content type | Review owner | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain-language explanation | Editor | Clarity, scope and example |
| Current product workflow | Product specialist | Official documentation and a controlled test |
| High-stakes or regulated guidance | Appropriate subject reviewer | Current primary evidence and an agreed approval |

## Common questions

### Does every AI-assisted sentence require disclosure?

Disclosure depends on the context and applicable editorial or platform requirements. Be transparent about methods where that helps readers understand how claims were produced or verified.

### Can fact-checking tools replace manual review?

They can help surface claims and sources, but a person should verify the original context and decide whether the evidence supports what will be published.

### Who has the final say when reviewers disagree?

Assign a publication owner before drafting. They should resolve factual disputes with evidence or remove the disputed claim if the team cannot support it.
