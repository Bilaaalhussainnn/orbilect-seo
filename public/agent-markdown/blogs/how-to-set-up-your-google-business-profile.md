## Check eligibility and find existing listings

Search Maps for your business before creating a profile. Claim an existing eligible listing through the ownership request if someone else manages it. Use the real customer-facing name and the category that describes your main activity. A service-area business should review whether its address should be visible.

### 1. Check your business type and find the right listing

A Google Business Profile gives eligible businesses a way to manage how their details appear on Google Search and Maps. Customers can check hours, find directions, call the business or follow a website or booking link. Eligibility generally requires in-person contact with customers during stated hours. A flower shop with a staffed storefront and a plumber who visits customer properties can both qualify. An online-only business without in-person service does not ordinarily qualify. A mailing address, PO box or rented virtual office is insufficient on its own.

Sign in with a business-controlled Google account. Search Maps for your exact name and city, then confirm the address and phone number. If the business already has an unverified listing, use the claim option. If another owner manages the verified listing, follow Google’s ownership request process. If the business is eligible and no listing exists, use business.google.com/add. Use the real-world business name and choose the most specific primary category that reflects the main activity. A service-area business that does not receive customers at its base should hide that address and enter its actual service areas.

## Complete and verify the profile

Add regular and special hours, the correct website, and customer-facing phone and service details. Upload current storefront, team or product photos where useful. Follow the verification method offered in your account and use the evidence it asks for, such as signage and proof you operate the business.

### 2. Complete the profile and prepare evidence for verification

Use the phone and website the business actually controls. Add normal hours, contact details, services, and special hours as available. Include an accurate description and choose additional categories only for work the business genuinely does. For a storefront, visitors should be able to recognise the entrance, signage and address. For a service-area business, keep the public service territory accurate and distinguish where the business operates from where it accepts walk-ins.

Open the profile while signed in and follow the verification choices Google displays. Possible methods include recorded video, live video, phone or text, email, and mail where offered. Google determines what is available and may require more than one method. For a recorded video, plan one unedited, continuous take of at least 30 seconds and record it in the mobile verification flow. Show the location and signs for a storefront, or nearby landmarks and work equipment for a service-area business. Include evidence that you manage the operation, such as unlocking a staff-only area or accessing branded work equipment. Keep faces and sensitive information out of the video. For mail or a code-based method, keep codes private and follow the account’s instructions before making changes to key details.

## Keep the information useful

Update holiday hours and test the link from mobile. If the profile is suspended, fix the underlying guideline issue and gather matching evidence before appealing. Ask customers for honest reviews without incentives. Separate calls, website visits and bookings when assessing results.

### 3. Review the live listing and resolve verification issues

If a video is rejected, open the notice, match each concern to the evidence requested, and record a replacement in the profile flow. If you are asked to verify again after a change, check that the new details are accurate before proceeding. If the profile is restricted, review the policy reason, correct the issue and gather evidence that matches the business name and details before appealing through Google. Avoid creating a duplicate to work around a restriction or ownership dispute.

Once verified, find the public profile on Search and Maps in a signed-out browser. Test the directions, phone, appointment and website links from a phone. Upload current images that show what customers will see or buy. Ask real customers for honest reviews without incentives and respond without revealing personal details. Keep special hours current. Use available Performance data to track actions such as calls or clicks, and compare the profile’s website traffic with identifiable website enquiries before drawing conclusions.

## Confirm eligibility before creating a profile

Check Google’s current eligibility guidance against how the business meets customers. Do not create extra profiles merely to target more locations. Establish the real business name, customer-facing address or service area, and who can complete the offered verification process.

## Choose categories around the actual service

Use a primary category that describes the core business and relevant additional categories when appropriate. Category choices should reflect what customers can genuinely buy. Avoid changing the business name to a string of keywords, even when competing listings appear to do so.

## Make booking and contact paths reliable

Test the website, call, directions, and booking links on a phone. Link to the relevant location page rather than an unrelated campaign page. Keep opening hours and special hours current so the profile and the destination website tell the same story.

## Request reviews through a consistent process

Invite customers to leave honest feedback without selecting only the happy ones or promising incentives. Assign someone to respond to concerns with discretion. Avoid disclosing private customer or patient details while explaining how the business can follow up.

## Read performance alongside real enquiries

Compare profile interactions with answered calls, bookings, and qualified enquiries. Keep a record of changes to hours, categories, and landing pages. A rise in interactions is useful context, but the commercial question is whether suitable customers reached the team.

## Prepare evidence for an ownership or verification request

Keep the business name, contact details and operating information consistent with the real business before starting verification. Gather the records or operational evidence relevant to the verification method Google actually offers; do not create details simply to fit the form.

Assign a business-controlled account as an owner and give other contributors suitable access. If a previous agency manages the listing, resolve ownership rather than creating a second profile that competes with the existing one.

## Plan for holiday hours and temporary operating changes

A profile can be technically complete and still disappoint customers when its hours are wrong. Make changes to holiday opening, appointment availability and service interruptions part of ordinary operations, with someone responsible for checking the website and listing together.

Recheck the customer journey after a change. A visitor who sees accurate hours but reaches a disconnected telephone number or an unavailable booking page still experiences inaccurate information about how the business can help.

[Google’s eligibility and address guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)

## Separate a public profile link from a review request link

Store the public listing URL and the review request URL as distinct business assets. A website's contact page may need the listing and directions, while a post-service message may need the direct review flow. Test both as a customer without relying on a management dashboard link.

Invite honest feedback without asking only satisfied customers or offering a reward. Keep the invitation neutral, and make it clear that customers should avoid including private project or personal details in a public review.

## Handle duplicate or outdated listings before expanding

Search for previous names, addresses and telephone numbers before opening another profile. A duplicate listing can split customer attention and make it harder to tell which information is current. Document the relevant URLs and use the appropriate Google support process for the situation.

Do not reuse a closed location's details for a different business merely to retain its reviews. The listing should describe the business that customers can actually contact and use today.

## How three local businesses handle verification

A bakery shows the street sign, storefront sign, and access to the till. A plumber who drives to clients hides the home-base address from the public profile and prepares evidence of work equipment and control of the business. A repair shop that also makes house calls uses its public storefront if it is staffed and customer-facing, then adds the genuine service area. Each case provides evidence of where the business operates and who is authorised to manage it.

After a manager leaves, transfer access using the profile’s people and access controls. Retain a business-controlled primary owner, grant managers the access they need and remove former providers once the transition is complete. Verify the public details after every change that could affect where and when customers can reach you.

## Common questions

### Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating, claiming and managing an eligible profile does not require an advertising subscription. You can link it to your website without running Google Ads.

### Can I choose a different verification method?

Use one of the options Google displays for your profile. If an offered method fails, follow the help flow or contact support when the profile does not present a practical option.

### Why can someone else see the profile while I cannot edit it?

A public listing can exist before you obtain management access. Check that you are signed into the right account and request ownership if an existing verified owner manages it.

### Is verification enough to rank for every nearby search?

Verification gives the business control over its listing details. Google says local visibility depends mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Maintain accurate details, build an informative website and collect honest feedback.

### Can I publish a registered business address where nobody serves customers?

Use Google’s eligibility and address guidelines. A mailing address alone does not establish a staffed location. If your team visits customers and people do not visit your address, set up an eligible service-area profile and hide the address.
