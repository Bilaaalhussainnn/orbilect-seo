## Check measurement before touching bids

Confirm what counts as a primary conversion and whether it genuinely matches the account’s objective. Test form, call and purchase tracking. Review conversion windows, duplicate events and whether campaigns optimise for a meaningful outcome.

### Audit the measurement before judging the campaign

Open the account and list the conversion actions used by each campaign. Check which actions contribute to bidding and whether they represent business outcomes. Test phone, form and purchase events where relevant. Review attribution windows, duplicate events and the match between credited conversions and accepted leads. Pull a period long enough to cover the purchase or sales cycle and note recent changes before drawing conclusions.

Audit targets in order of dependence

1. **Confirm tracking**Test which goals bidding uses
2. **Inspect delivery**Review real search terms, costs and destinations
3. **Prioritise fixes**Record changes and compare later business outcomes

## Inspect where the budget went

Review search terms, targeting, locations, match types and ads by campaign objective. Look for irrelevant terms and separate brand demand from new-customer campaigns. Compare traffic and cost with actual lead or order quality, not just click volume.

### Review intent, campaign structure and spend

Inspect search terms to see what triggered search ads. Separate ad group keywords from actual queries. Review query relevance, location settings, budgets and performance by campaign type. Compare how brand and non-brand searches perform and check that negative keywords do not block important queries. Test the ad-to-page journey on a phone and verify that the same offer and eligibility details are visible.

## Prioritise and document changes

Fix measurement or major leakage before testing creative. Record the date, campaign and hypothesis for each change. Leave enough observation time to judge a decision, particularly when sales take days or weeks to close.

### Prioritise fixes and set a review window

Rank findings by cost, confidence and implementation effort. Broken measurement and wasted spend usually deserve attention before rewriting assets with little data. Make changes in a sequence your team can monitor. Record the date, owner and outcome of each change. Check impressions and spend soon after release, then use a longer review window for lead quality and sales.

## Start with the conversion inventory

List every conversion action and decide whether it represents a business outcome or a supporting interaction. Check primary and secondary treatment in the current account. Verify the website implementation before evaluating campaign performance, since bidding decisions may depend on these signals.

## Review queries against the actual offer

Read search terms in the context of what the business can deliver. Identify irrelevant intent, accidental broad coverage, and useful demand that lacks a matching landing page. Keep an evidence log so proposed exclusions can be reviewed before removing potentially valuable traffic.

## Check geography and scheduling assumptions

Confirm the markets served, location options, languages, and times when enquiries can be handled. Compare them with the sales team’s actual coverage. A campaign can generate valid clicks from people the business cannot serve profitably or respond to promptly.

## Inspect destination pages and follow-up

Test the final URLs, mobile experience, forms, call links, and response process. Follow an enquiry into the CRM where access permits. Campaign optimization has limited value when leads are lost between the website and the person responsible for contacting them.

## Prioritize fixes by risk and impact

Separate broken measurement, wasted spend, weak relevance, and experiments. Give each recommendation an owner and a verification method. Implement urgent reliability fixes first, then compare performance over an appropriate window instead of changing every setting at once.

## Separate brand demand from new customer discovery

Review branded and non-branded search activity separately where the account structure and reporting allow it. Existing customers searching the business name have a different context from people comparing unfamiliar providers.

Do not assume that one group's efficiency represents the whole account. Record how each campaign contributes to the business objective and look for inappropriate overlap, unclear destinations or reporting choices that hide where new qualified demand is actually coming from.

## Check the conversion action used for bidding

Inspect which actions the campaign treats as meaningful outcomes and confirm that their implementation still matches the business goal. A historic test event, page visit or duplicate form signal can influence decisions if it remains configured as a valuable conversion.

Complete a realistic customer journey and follow the corresponding data. Document any proposed change before applying it so later performance comparisons account for a different measurement definition rather than attributing everything to the advertising strategy.

## Read search queries with the service team

Review a representative set of queries alongside someone who understands what the business actually sells. Identify requests that are outside scope, research-only searches and wording that reveals a genuine customer need.

Use those findings to improve targeting and landing-page information through a controlled change process. Do not exclude a query solely because it has not converted in a tiny sample; consider its relevance, intent and the quality of the destination first.

## Leave an audit trail that another operator can follow

For each recommendation, record the affected campaign or page, evidence, proposed action, owner and verification step. Distinguish urgent measurement or access problems from experiments that need a period of observation.

Keep an export or appropriate record of the starting configuration before major edits. A useful audit should enable careful implementation and later explanation, rather than leaving the next operator with a list of unexplained changes and no way to interpret the results.

## A dealership audit example

A showroom sees a large volume of conversions, but the account counts map clicks as leads. The team makes the successful test-drive request primary for campaigns where that is the goal. It reports navigation clicks separately, reviews generic parts queries and tracks confirmed appointments in the CRM.

Review operational gaps that do not show up under campaign performance. Check account access, billing contacts, Merchant Center connections where relevant and whether staff know who approves a budget change. Find sudden changes in the account history and check which were automated, which were manual and what the owner expected to happen. An audit should produce a prioritised work list with owners and success measures, so teammates can verify the changes after the audit ends.

Sort findings before making edits

| Finding | Potential impact | First move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page views count as leads | Bidding may optimise for the wrong action | Test and correct the conversion definition |
| High spend on unrelated queries | Budget misses the intended buyer | Review matching and add carefully chosen exclusions |
| Ad promise differs from landing page | Qualified visitors may leave | Align copy and the live destination |

## Common questions

### Should I pause every campaign with poor ROAS?

First check whether the tracking, conversion delay and comparison period make sense. Prioritise clear waste and investigate campaigns with weak evidence before pausing broadly.

### Can recommendations replace an audit?

Use recommendations as inputs. Review each against campaign objectives, business data and the constraints of the account.

### How far back should the audit go?

Use enough data to cover the conversion lag and normal buying cycle. Compare a recent period with a representative earlier one and document promotions, outages and tracking changes.
