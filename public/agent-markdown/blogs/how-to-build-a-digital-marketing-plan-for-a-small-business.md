## Define the audience and commercial outcome

Write down which buyers, needs and locations you serve and what a useful outcome looks like. Set a baseline for leads, purchases and current channel costs before deciding where to invest.

### Set a business target and identify your constraint

Write down the service, margin, region and customer segment you can serve well. Define the number of accepted bookings or sales you need and what capacity you have. Review current sources, budget, seasonality and customer questions. Choose a primary goal for the planning period and a small number of activities you can deliver with the people available.

## Choose a limited set of experiments

Map the key pages you need and select one or two channels you can support. Write down budget, responsibilities, and what would justify continuing. An initial plan might test one service page, one paid campaign, and follow-up emails to relevant leads.

### Give each campaign a page, an owner and a measurement

Start with the channel most likely to reach the buyers you want. Build or improve the page a campaign needs, then verify the phone, form or cart before buying traffic. Allocate a limited test budget and define the lead or order quality you expect. Decide who will publish content, reply to enquiries and review outcomes. Record the reasons for choosing a channel so a later change is deliberate.

## Review the plan regularly

Compare actual results with the baseline. Look at qualified outcomes and operational capacity, not just traffic. Stop activities that lack a credible path to the goal and use what customers ask to improve future pages and campaigns.

### Review a short reporting cycle and revise the plan

Compare actual demand, completed work and customer outcomes with your target. Identify whether weak results come from poor traffic, a confusing page, slow sales response or a supply constraint. Keep or increase activities supported by evidence and retire those that repeatedly miss the target. Record the next test and use a longer period where the buying cycle requires it.

## Choose a measurable commercial goal

Define the customers, offer, and result the plan should support. Use a goal the business can verify, such as qualified enquiries for a specific service. Avoid making the plan a list of channels without explaining how those activities connect to sales or service capacity.

## Match activity to available capacity

Account for the time needed to create content, answer enquiries, fulfil work, and maintain the website. A small team benefits from a focused channel mix it can operate consistently. More campaigns are not automatically useful if follow-up and delivery become unreliable.

## Create a realistic budget model

Separate media spend, production, tools, and management costs. State assumptions about lead qualification and sales conversion. Use a labelled example to explore affordability rather than presenting a universal marketing percentage as the right answer for every business.

## Plan a short learning cycle

Choose a few specific tests with a hypothesis, owner, date, and success measure. Keep enough consistency to interpret the results. Review the actual customer response before expanding the plan or committing to a large volume of content and advertising.

## Build reporting into ordinary operations

Keep a simple view of spend, qualified enquiries, response time, opportunities, and revenue where available. Use the review to decide what to continue, change, or stop. A useful plan remains editable as evidence arrives instead of becoming a document that is never revisited.

## Write a capacity-aware offer before selecting channels

Confirm what the business can deliver, who the service fits and how new enquiries will be handled. Include real limits on time, geography or available stock before producing a campaign that may promise more than operations can support.

Then select channels that can reach the relevant audience with a clear message. The plan should connect marketing activity to a deliverable service, not merely produce a schedule of posts and advertisements without an agreed commercial purpose.

## Separate a learning budget from an ongoing commitment

Describe what a small initial campaign is intended to reveal and which costs are included. Record the decisions it can inform, such as demand, message clarity or the suitability of a landing page.

Do not treat a limited test as proof of long-term performance. Use the findings to refine the next step, and distinguish setup work, reusable content and recurring media costs when deciding whether an activity should become part of the ordinary marketing budget.

## Create a content plan from repeated sales questions

Ask the customer-facing team which questions delay a decision, disqualify an enquiry or require the same explanation repeatedly. Organize those questions into useful service-page improvements and supporting content.

Give each planned item an audience, purpose and next action. This produces a more practical editorial calendar than publishing frequently without knowing which customer need the new article, email or video is supposed to answer.

## Run a monthly decision review with a short agenda

Review what was delivered, what suitable customers did, what the team learned and which issue should be addressed next. Bring sales and service feedback alongside marketing reports so the plan reflects the whole customer journey.

Keep the resulting actions small enough to assign and complete. A plan becomes useful through consistent decisions and follow-through, rather than through repeatedly replacing the strategy whenever a platform launches a new format or feature.

## A ninety-day plan for a local contractor

In the first month the owner fixes intake and improves two core service pages. In month two the team tests search ads for the most profitable work. In month three it reviews accepted jobs and after-hours response. The owner changes coverage and callback rules before increasing the budget.

Keep a simple plan for what you will stop when demand changes. If the team cannot reply quickly or has reached capacity, slow the paid test and improve intake before adding another channel. If the website attracts the right audience but enquiries fail to complete, work on the offer and conversion path. A short list of accountable activities gives the business a way to shift work without losing the measurement record.

## Common questions

### Do I need to market on every channel?

Choose channels you can serve with useful content and reliable follow-up. Expand once you understand which activities work and can continue them without compromising quality.

### What if my budget is very small?

Start with the website, accepted enquiries and the channel that already produces buyer interest. Test with a limited scope and compare time as well as money.

### How often should a small business adjust its plan?

Review operational signals weekly and revisit strategic choices over a period that fits your sales cycle. Avoid changing several variables because of one quiet day.
