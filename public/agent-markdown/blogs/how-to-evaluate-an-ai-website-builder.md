## Compare tools using the same brief

Give each builder the same audience, pages, content and required task. Compare how easily the team can change navigation, edit layouts and maintain repeatable pages after the first draft.

### Write a practical brief and compare equivalent builds

Test candidate builders with the same requirements: page inventory, forms, integrations, who edits, and which content must be portable. Build a homepage, a reusable content detail page and a complete conversion path. A compelling first mockup is useful, but maintainers need to know how templates and content behave after launch.

## Check the rendered page

Test mobile layout, keyboard navigation, text contrast, headings, forms, metadata and performance. Verify canonical URLs and redirects where relevant. Check export options, ownership and what happens when the subscription ends.

### Inspect the published site, not just the editor canvas

Open a public test page and inspect redirects, meaningful rendered text, links, headings and mobile layout. Check page metadata, index directives and sitemap controls. Complete keyboard navigation, test a failure state in your form and measure page performance with representative imagery. If custom code is necessary, confirm its scope and who will support it.

## Test ongoing publishing

Make a realistic change, such as publishing a new location page or updating shared pricing. Record how much work it takes and whether content can be reviewed before release. Choose based on the whole publishing workflow.

### Evaluate ownership, operating costs and handover

Compare plan limits, who owns the domain and whether staff can export content or deploy elsewhere. Check what happens to payments, member accounts or form data if you cancel. Ask an editor to update a representative page and ask a second person to restore or roll it back. Keep the builder whose constraints fit how the site will be operated.

## Test with your real content

Use actual service descriptions, long titles, images, and forms in a small trial. A polished generated homepage may conceal weak secondary pages. Evaluate whether the tool can maintain a coherent system as content grows rather than judging only the first screenshot.

## Check ownership and portability

Confirm what can be exported, how the domain is controlled, and what happens when the subscription ends. Review database and asset access if the tool builds an application. Distinguish source-code ownership from permission to use a hosted design.

## Verify operational features

Test enquiry storage, email delivery, authentication, and payments where applicable. Confirm how secrets and environment variables are managed. A generated success screen does not prove that a backend action completed, so inspect the resulting record or provider response.

## Inspect accessibility and loading

Review keyboard navigation, contrast, headings, image dimensions, and mobile overflow. Measure a published page with real assets and integrations. Ask how the tool handles responsive image sizes and unnecessary scripts instead of relying solely on a marketing speed score.

## Calculate the cost of maintenance

Include hosting, model credits, plugins, revisions, and specialist help. Test whether a routine content edit breaks another section. Choose the tool that fits the team’s ability to maintain and verify the site after the initial generation is complete.

## Ask the tool to complete an ordinary maintenance task

After generating the first page, try changing a service, adding a longer heading and updating a shared contact detail. Check whether the change remains consistent across the relevant pages and whether the editor can explain what it modified.

This is a more useful ownership test than judging the first screenshot alone. A website that looks polished but is difficult to maintain may create more work every time the business needs an ordinary update.

## Check what the published output actually contains

Inspect the real page title, main content, links and loading behavior after publication. Confirm that the deployment uses the intended domain and that important pages have useful, directly reachable URLs.

Do not infer technical quality from the builder's preview. A convincing mockup is different from a public website with functioning navigation, sensible index controls, accessible forms and content that remains available when decorative effects are absent.

## Follow business integrations through their failure states

Test a form, booking or payment connection beyond the attractive front-end screen. Check where the information arrives, what confirms success and what the visitor sees when the external service does not respond.

Decide who owns configuration and support for each integration. An AI builder can accelerate interface creation, but the business still needs a working operational process and appropriate access to diagnose a failed submission or disconnected account.

## Evaluate the exit path before committing to a platform

Review the available export, domain, asset and content arrangements under the plan being considered. Distinguish a downloadable image of a page from editable source or content that another maintainer can actually use.

Record the recurring costs and the work required to move later. Portability is not identical across builders, so assess the documented capabilities of the specific product rather than assuming that all AI-generated websites can be transferred in the same way.

## Two builders, one business test

A consultant asks both tools to create a services page, a portfolio collection and a quote form. One preview looks stronger but requires manual work to edit case studies. The other produces a reusable template and tested email delivery. The team selects based on publishing and maintenance as well as appearance.

Bring the people who will run the site into the trial. Ask an editor to revise a service description, update a blog image and publish a time-sensitive notice. Ask someone else to inspect the contact destination and restore an older page. Measure time and permissions needed for each task. A builder deserves credit for reducing recurring work, not simply producing a convincing homepage during a guided demo.

## Common questions

### Will AI-generated code make the site perform poorly?

Measure the actual page after adding real content. Check large assets, third-party scripts and the most important mobile interactions.

### Do I need an AI builder if I already have a CMS?

Compare the improvement in editing, maintenance or speed against migration work. A CMS may already support the business well.

### Can I compare builders using their default demo content?

Use the same realistic assets and requirements for each. Long headlines, obscure form errors and genuine data are more useful than a polished demo for identifying tradeoffs.
