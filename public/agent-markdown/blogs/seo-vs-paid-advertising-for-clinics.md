## Separate local visibility from immediate demand

Clear service and location pages help patients find relevant information over time. Paid campaigns may reach people quickly in targeted areas, subject to platform policies and applicable rules. Compare both based on suitable appointments and capacity.

### Separate patient information needs from acquisition targets

A clinic website should help patients verify services, practitioners, hours and booking options. Put accurate practitioner and clinic details on appropriate pages and use qualified clinical review for medical statements. Organic search can support ongoing discovery of confirmed services, while paid campaigns can help advertise a defined appointment type where platform policy and local rules allow it. Start with the services your clinic can actually deliver.

## Build trust into the booking path

Make practitioners, services, address, hours and next steps clear. Have an appropriate reviewer check medical content. Make appointment instructions accessible and treat patient details carefully when setting up analytics or ad measurement.

### Choose a limited, reviewable advertising test

Confirm the ad platform’s healthcare policy, targeting restrictions and the jurisdictions involved before launch. Give a named owner responsibility for checking copy and destinations. Avoid claiming outcomes you cannot support. Use terms that match public information on the site and direct visitors to the appropriate booking path. Decide what the clinic will do if an appointment is unavailable before adding spend.

## Compare booked appointments and operational fit

Track call and booking completions rather than visits alone. Use appropriately configured reporting that avoids exposing patient information. If enquiries rise but appointments do not, check availability, call handling and the match between the service and campaign.

### Measure booking activity with a privacy review

Map which services touch appointment or contact information before enabling analytics or advertising tags. Discuss with qualified privacy staff whether a tag can be used on a particular page and what data it can receive. Review vendor terms, URLs, form payloads and the use of audience tools. Track aggregate scheduling capacity and confirmed appointments through approved systems, and avoid passing patient details in marketing links or event parameters.

## Separate service education from appointment intent

A patient researching a procedure may need a clear explanation before choosing a clinic. Build accurate service information and an accessible appointment route. Use paid campaigns only with appropriate targeting, claims, and platform eligibility checks for the specific service and jurisdiction.

## Give clinical claims a named review process

Have qualified people review medical descriptions, risks, and suitability statements. Cite authoritative clinical sources and record review dates. Marketing copy should help a patient understand options without promising outcomes or replacing an individual assessment by an appropriate professional.

## Respect privacy throughout the funnel

Review forms, analytics parameters, and advertising integrations for sensitive information. Collect only what the appointment workflow needs and explain the next step. Obtain appropriate specialist advice on applicable rules rather than assuming a generic tracking setup is suitable for patient journeys.

## Compare attended appointments rather than clicks

Measure valid enquiries, appointment bookings, attendance, and service capacity with appropriate permissions. A campaign can generate bookings that the clinic cannot serve. Compare channels using consistent time windows and distinguish new-patient demand from existing patients looking for practical information.

## Maintain a balanced acquisition plan

Use organic pages for reliable service explanations and campaigns for defined, eligible demand. Review waiting times, budget, and staffing before scaling. Both channels depend on accurate information, a usable mobile journey, and a team able to handle the resulting enquiries responsibly.

## Check policy eligibility before producing a campaign

Healthcare advertising requirements can depend on the service, location, targeting and certification involved. Review the current platform policy for the specific offer rather than copying a campaign used by a different clinic or assuming every medical service is treated alike.

Keep the policy review with the campaign brief and route clinical wording through the clinic's designated reviewer. This reduces avoidable rework and prevents advertising promises from drifting beyond what the service can appropriately explain or deliver.

## Keep appointment forms separate from advertising payloads

Review what information forms, URLs and marketing tags transmit. A patient-facing journey should not casually expose symptoms, appointment reasons or contact details through tracking parameters and general analytics events.

Use an appropriate booking or clinical system for information that belongs there, and involve the clinic's privacy and technical owners in the design. Marketing measurement can focus on suitable aggregate outcomes without copying private appointment content into an advertising dashboard.

[Google Ads: Health in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/16701855)

## Align availability with the services being promoted

Ask the operations team whether the advertised service has appointment capacity, relevant staff and a workable response process. A campaign can generate interest while making the patient experience worse if enquiries arrive for unavailable sessions.

Update the page and campaign when availability changes. Explain the next step accurately, distinguish an appointment request from a confirmed booking and avoid implying that submitting a form guarantees access to a particular clinician or time.

[Google Ads: Healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031)

## Compare patient journeys using consistent operational definitions

Define what counts as an enquiry, a booked appointment and an attended appointment before comparing organic and paid channels. Keep those measures distinct and use a reporting approach appropriate to the clinic's handling requirements.

Review cancellations, response delays and service suitability alongside acquisition activity. The useful question is whether the channels help appropriate patients reach an appropriate service, not simply which source reports more clicks or the cheapest initial form submission.

## A clinic opening new physiotherapy appointments

The clinic checks whether it can support existing patients and new referrals. It publishes an accurate service page and updates its public office details. It considers a limited ad test only after reviewing eligibility, destination content and measurement. The team monitors appointment capacity and accepted bookings before expanding the budget.

Agree on who will update public details when services or availability change. A campaign can continue attracting requests even when the clinic no longer has the capacity to accept them. Provide a clear alternative contact method for administrative questions and publish accurate hours. Before expanding a limited test, have staff assess whether enquiries reached the right team and matched the service that was advertised, using appropriately reviewed and aggregated information.

## Common questions

### Can a clinic use remarketing for any visitor?

Health-related audience use can be restricted by platform policy and privacy obligations. Have the proposed campaign and data flow reviewed before using these features.

### Should a website report all patient bookings to ad platforms?

Start with measurement the clinic can lawfully and appropriately use. Business totals can be valuable without exporting sensitive individual-level information.

### Which pages should the clinic improve first?

Prioritise service, facility and practitioner pages that already support patient decisions. Check that the information remains current and has the necessary clinical or operational review.
