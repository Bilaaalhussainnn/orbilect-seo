## Use AI to organise actual search evidence

Collect queries from Search Console, support conversations, paid campaigns and direct customer interviews. Ask AI to group those phrases by need and buying stage. Use its suggestions as hypotheses and check demand with independent data.

### Gather evidence before asking for keyword ideas

Start with Search Console queries, real sales or support questions, campaign search terms where available and competitor page formats. Strip private customer information before sharing examples with any external tool. Ask an assistant to group terms by problem and expected page type. Treat suggested search volumes, rankings and trends as hypotheses unless you can verify them independently.

## Review results before mapping pages

Inspect representative search results to see what types of pages serve each task. Merge variants that share intent and separate topics that need different answers. Map each group to an existing page or a clearly defined new one.

### Validate the clusters with search results and customer context

Spot-check the queries in the markets you serve. Look for different kinds of pages and decide what the searcher expects to accomplish. Merge near-synonyms when one page serves the same job and separate terms with different underlying intent. Note where an apparent high-volume opportunity has poor commercial fit or lacks evidence from the audience.

## Refresh the map as you learn

Review which pages earn clicks and whether those visitors qualify. Update the map when products change, new questions appear, or search results shift. Keep a record of the evidence used for each change.

### Turn the shortlist into a page map and review it

Record the intended URL, audience, page type, next action and supporting source for each useful group. Mark whether you will improve an existing page or create a new one. After publication, compare query coverage, qualified traffic and outcomes. Revise the group when real results show that searchers use a phrase differently from the model’s suggestion.

## Start with real customer vocabulary

Gather support questions, sales objections, site search terms, and existing search queries. Use AI to organize these inputs into possible themes. Generated keyword suggestions are hypotheses until demand, intent, and business relevance are checked against evidence.

## Separate discovery from volume claims

Do not treat a model’s suggested search volume or difficulty as measured data unless a named current source supports it. Record the tool, market, and date for estimates. Use ranges and limitations honestly when the available evidence is incomplete.

## Review search intent manually

Inspect representative results and identify what a useful page would need to answer. Distinguish an informational question from a comparison or purchase task. Several similar phrases may belong on one strong page rather than separate pages with lightly rewritten introductions.

## Map topics to existing assets

Compare the proposed cluster with current pages before commissioning content. Improve an existing page when it already serves the need. Record internal linking and ownership so the plan strengthens the site instead of creating competing pages and maintenance work.

## Validate the plan after publication

Review impressions, relevant visits, engagement with the next step, and qualified outcomes. Compare the observed queries with the original hypothesis. Use the results to refine the topic map rather than assuming the initial AI-generated list was a final research product.

## Keep model suggestions separate from observed demand

Label whether a phrase comes from a customer conversation, Search Console, a keyword tool or an AI-generated suggestion. Those inputs have different evidential value and should not be merged into an apparently verified demand table.

Use AI to propose language and organize possibilities, then validate important decisions with the available search and business evidence. A fluent explanation of a term does not establish its search volume, competition or suitability for your offer.

## Review ambiguous terms before clustering them

Ask whether similar wording describes the same task. A search for a software template, a managed service and a training course may share keywords while requiring different information and destinations.

Inspect representative results and compare the intent with what your business provides. Keep doubtful terms in a review group rather than letting an automated cluster assign them to a page simply because their language appears similar.

## Turn a keyword group into an editorial brief

For each approved group, describe the audience, main question, suitable page type, useful evidence and next action. Record which existing page owns the topic before asking a writer or AI tool to draft anything.

This gives the content a purpose beyond including a list of phrases. A good brief also explains what the page should leave out, helping avoid overlap with another service or guide that already serves the neighbouring question.

## Revisit the map using real performance and sales feedback

After publication, compare incoming queries and useful customer actions with the intended purpose of the page. Ask the team whether new enquiries match the service described or reveal an important missing explanation.

Refine the brief and page map using that evidence. Do not continuously replace the strategy with a fresh batch of AI suggestions; preserve the record of what was tried and what the business learned from the resulting visitors.

## Keyword research for a B2B cleaning service

A team provides actual query data and asks an assistant to sort it by property type and buying stage. It confirms that “office cleaning checklist” leads to planning material and “commercial office cleaning” leads to service providers. It improves one service page and adds a genuinely informative checklist that links back when relevant.

Keep a note about each term’s origin in your brief. A query from Search Console tells you something different from a phrase a competitor uses or a suggestion from a customer interview. When several sources point to the same need, design an article or service page around that task. If the evidence conflicts, review current results and talk to people who use the service before investing in a new template or series.

## Common questions

### Can an AI assistant supply accurate search volume?

Use a dependable data source for estimates and record the source, market and period. A model can organise numbers you provide, but a generated number alone is not evidence.

### Should I create a page for every suggested variation?

Group close variants around the same customer task. Publish separate pages only when you can serve meaningfully different needs.

### How should I handle local or multilingual keyword groups?

Check search results and wording in each target market. Translation alone may not capture what customers mean, which service they expect or which city they consider relevant.
