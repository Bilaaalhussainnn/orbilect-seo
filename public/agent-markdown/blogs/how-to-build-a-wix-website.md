## Set up the site around a clear offer

Choose an editor and design suited to the content. Build the main service, product and contact pages, then make pricing or booking details easy to find. Check the mobile layout before cloning a template or adding more pages.

### Design around the site’s main task

Choose which actions the site will support: request an appointment, make a purchase or get a quote. Give those tasks clear pages and short paths from navigation. Select an editor and template that the team can maintain. Create consistent typography and button styles, then replace placeholder text with the actual service terms, delivery area and contact details.

## Use the built-in SEO setup

Connect the correct domain, review the SEO setup for important pages, and verify the property in Search Console. Check the generated sitemap and whether the URLs people should discover are included. Add apps for clearly defined needs and test their effect on loading and forms.

### Use site-wide settings and review important pages individually

Set the primary domain and check HTTPS. Configure page titles, descriptions, readable slugs and a meaningful H1. Review settings for products, bookings or dynamic pages where relevant. Follow the SEO Setup Checklist for project-level tasks and inspect the public sitemap after publishing. Define redirects when changing a live slug. Check which pages you actually want crawled before using bulk changes.

## Run a launch rehearsal

Test mobile navigation, enquiry notifications and booking or checkout flows. Inspect the published domain without being logged in. If moving from an earlier site, test redirects for the URLs that brought customers or had incoming links.

### Test the live workflow under visitor conditions

Open the site in a signed-out session and use a phone. Try menu links, forms, mobile navigation, payment or booking, and any confirmation emails. Check that links point to the final destinations rather than preview URLs. Then inspect a representative homepage, service and booking page in Search Console and record changes that still need work.

## Choose the editing workflow first

Confirm whether the chosen editor suits the team’s maintenance skills and the layout requirements. Prepare the site map and actual content before customizing every screen. Keep the business account in control of the domain, billing, and administrator permissions.

## Design the mobile journey separately

Inspect menus, text wrapping, image crops, and enquiry buttons on a phone. Do not assume the automatic mobile arrangement is the finished design. Test the same customer task on desktop and mobile and remove steps that add friction without useful information.

## Use applications selectively

Add an app when it solves a defined requirement and check its effect on loading, billing, and data handling. Avoid overlapping booking, marketing, or form tools unless the integration is deliberate. Review which system becomes the source of truth for each enquiry.

## Review search settings with real pages

Check titles, descriptions, canonical settings, and sitemap visibility on the published domain. Inspect representative pages rather than relying only on a setup checklist. A completed checklist supports administration but cannot establish that a page answers a customer’s search well.

## Verify the operational handover

Test forms, automated notifications, booking availability, and payment paths where applicable. Confirm who receives account recovery messages and service renewals. Record these responsibilities so the business can keep operating the site after its original builder leaves.

## Check the difference between editor and customer views

Review the published website in a clean browser session, on a phone and with keyboard navigation. Editor previews are useful during production, but they do not replace testing the actual domain and the complete route into a form or booking.

Include long page titles and realistic images in the test. A layout that works with placeholder content can behave differently when a business publishes its actual services, terms and location information.

## Preserve important URLs during a rebuild

Record the pages that customers already reach through search, saved links and directory profiles before restructuring the site. Where a permanent URL change is necessary, review the platform's current redirect controls and choose the closest useful replacement.

Test the old destination after launch and correct internal links as well. Sending every retired page to the homepage makes it harder for a returning visitor to continue the task that brought them there.

## Review the effect of added apps on the customer journey

For every app, identify the business problem it solves and the pages on which it needs to run. Compare that benefit with its recurring cost, additional loading work and maintenance responsibilities.

Test the complete published journey after adding or removing an integration. A chat tool, booking widget or marketing script should not cover the primary button, delay essential content or create conflicting requests for the same customer information.

## Keep business ownership separate from agency access

Confirm that the business can recover its website account, domain and connected services without relying on one external contributor's personal login. Give collaborators the access their role needs and document the relevant renewal responsibilities.

A practical handover also includes forms, analytics and the ordinary publishing workflow. This reduces the risk that a small content change or staff departure becomes an avoidable interruption to customer enquiries.

## A Wix booking site example

A personal training studio publishes one service page per coaching format. The booking widget is tested for availability, cancellations and customer emails. The team keeps useful service information visible outside the widget, so readers can check suitability without starting a booking.

A redesign is a good moment to consolidate duplicate pages. Export a list of current URLs and identify the pages getting meaningful traffic or referrals. Preserve addresses with a clear role, replace obsolete pages with useful alternatives when possible, and test redirects in the public version. Review the display of structured data and SEO templates for products or bookings rather than assuming the homepage settings apply to every page.

## Common questions

### Can Wix sites appear in Google search?

Yes, eligible public pages can be indexed. Confirm the site is published and publicly reachable and review site and page indexing settings.

### Should every booking or filter page be indexed?

Only expose pages that give searchers useful public content. Keep private workflow steps and redundant variations out of your intended search inventory.

### How should I improve a slow-looking Wix page?

Check the actual page on a representative phone. Look for large background media, widgets and long-loading embeds, then test whether removing each changes the time until visitors can use the page.
